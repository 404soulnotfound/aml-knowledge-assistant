# FATF Updated Guidance for a Risk-Based Approach to Virtual Assets and VASPs

## Definition of Virtual Assets (VAs)
A virtual asset is a digital representation of value that can be digitally traded, or transferred, and can be used for payment or investment purposes. Virtual assets do not include digital representations of fiat currencies, securities, and other financial assets already covered elsewhere in the FATF Recommendations.

## Core VASP Obligations
Virtual Asset Service Providers (VASPs) encompass any natural or legal person who conducts one or more of the following activities for or on behalf of another natural or legal person:
1. Exchange between virtual assets and fiat currencies.
2. Exchange between one or more forms of virtual assets.
3. Transfer of virtual assets.
4. Safekeeping and/or administration of virtual assets or instruments enabling control over virtual assets (custodial wallet providers).
5. Participation in and provision of financial services related to an issuer’s offer and/or sale of a virtual asset.

## The Crypto Travel Rule (Recommendation 16 for VAs)
The Travel Rule requires ordering and beneficiary VASPs and financial institutions to obtain and hold required and accurate originator information and required beneficiary information on virtual asset transfers, and make it available on request to appropriate authorities.
- Immediate transmission: Originator and beneficiary details must be submitted immediately and securely alongside the crypto transaction or off-chain protocol.
- Sunrise Issue: Differences in regulatory timelines across jurisdictions ("sunrise issue") do not exempt VASPs from complying; VASPs must apply enhanced due diligence and counterparty VASP due diligence before transacting with unregulated entities.
- Unhosted Wallets: When transacting with unhosted (non-custodial/self-hosted) wallets, VASPs must assess the counterparty risk using blockchain analytics and apply risk-based measures, such as verifying ownership or restricting transfer amounts.

## High-Risk Red Flags in Virtual Assets
- Structuring transactions just under the USD/EUR 1,000 Travel Rule threshold.
- Frequent transfers to or from known mixing/tumbling services, privacy coin protocols (e.g. Monero, Tornado Cash), or darknet marketplaces.
- Rapid movement of funds across multiple blockchains within short timeframes (chain-hopping).
- Interaction with peer-to-peer (P2P) platforms operating in jurisdictions with weak or non-existent AML/CFT controls.
