# FATF Guidance: The 40 Recommendations (Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing)

## Recommendation 1: Assessing Risks & Applying a Risk-Based Approach (RBA)
Countries and financial institutions should identify, assess, and understand the money laundering and terrorist financing risks for the country and the sectors, and should apply resources and measures aimed at mitigating those risks effectively. Higher-risk areas require enhanced measures (EDD), while simplified measures (SDD) may be permitted for demonstrated low-risk scenarios.

## Recommendation 10: Customer Due Diligence (CDD)
Financial institutions should be prohibited from keeping anonymous accounts or accounts in fictitious names. Financial institutions should undertake CDD measures when:
1. Establishing business relations.
2. Carrying out occasional transactions above the applicable designated threshold (USD/EUR 15,000) or wire transfers above USD/EUR 1,000.
3. There is a suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing, regardless of any thresholds or exemptions.
4. The financial institution has doubts about the veracity or adequacy of previously obtained customer identification data.

### Verification of Beneficial Ownership
Financial institutions must identify the customer and verify that customer’s identity using reliable, independent source documents, data, or information. For legal persons and arrangements, financial institutions must identify the beneficial owner(s) who ultimately own or control 25% or more of the shares, voting rights, or ownership interest, or exercise controlling influence.

## Recommendation 12: Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)
Financial institutions should, in relation to foreign PEPs (individuals entrusted with prominent public functions by a foreign country, such as heads of state, senior politicians, senior judicial or military officials, senior executives of state-owned corporations):
1. Implement appropriate risk-management systems to determine whether the customer or beneficial owner is a PEP.
2. Obtain senior management approval for establishing or continuing business relationships with such customers.
3. Take reasonable measures to establish the source of wealth and source of funds.
4. Conduct enhanced ongoing monitoring of the business relationship.
For domestic PEPs or persons entrusted with a prominent function by an international organisation, institutions must adopt these measures in higher-risk business relationships.

## Recommendation 16: Wire Transfers & The Travel Rule
Countries should ensure that financial institutions include required and accurate originator information, and required beneficiary information, on wire transfers and related messages throughout the payment chain.
- Threshold: Applies to wire transfers over USD/EUR 1,000.
- Originator information required: Name, account number (or unique transaction reference number), and address, national identity number, customer identification number, or date and place of birth.
- Beneficiary information required: Name and account number (or unique transaction identifier).

## Recommendation 20: Reporting of Suspicious Transactions (STR)
If a financial institution suspects or has reasonable grounds to suspect that funds are the proceeds of a criminal activity, or are related to terrorist financing, it should be required by law to report its suspicions promptly to the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU).
- Tipping-Off: Financial institutions, their directors, officers, and employees should be strictly prohibited by law from disclosing ("tipping-off") the fact that an STR or related information is being reported to the FIU.

## Recommendation 15: New Technologies & Virtual Asset Service Providers (VASPs)
Countries and financial institutions should identify and assess the money laundering or terrorist financing risks that may arise in relation to the development of new products and new business practices, including new delivery mechanisms, and the use of new or developing technologies for both new and pre-existing products.
VASPs must be licensed or registered and subjected to effective systems for monitoring and ensuring compliance with the FATF Recommendations, including the Travel Rule for crypto transfers exceeding USD/EUR 1,000.
